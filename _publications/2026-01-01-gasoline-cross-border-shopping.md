excerpt: 'This paper discusses the limitations of a standard test for cross-border shopping (CBS): price convergence at the border. Using a novel station-level panel of prices and quantities for the universe of Argentine gasoline stations, I exploit a 2015 tax reform that expanded a tax-exempt zone and cut the applicable excise from 55.8% to 0%---a roughly 30% reduction in retail prices under full pass-through. Quantities sold rose 22% at exempted stations and fell 7% at non-exempted stations just across the new border, with effects concentrated close to the border. Yet pass-through is spatially uniform within each region, indicating no price convergence despite substantial CBS. Seven years later, firms on the high-tax side of the border experience a 10-percentage-point decline in survival probability relative to exempted stations.'
