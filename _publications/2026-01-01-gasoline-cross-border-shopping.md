excerpt: 'This paper discusses the limits of relying on price data to detect and measure cross-border shopping (CBS), since a standard prediction is that the pass-through of a tax should vary with distance to the jurisdictional border. Using a novel station-level panel of prices and quantities for the universe of Argentine gasoline stations, I exploit a 2015 tax reform that expanded a southern tax-exempt zone and cut the applicable excise from 55% to 5%---a roughly 30% reduction in retail prices. Quantities sold rose 14% at exempted stations and fell 8% at non-exempted stations just north of the new border, with effects concentrated close to the border. Yet pass-through is spatially uniform within each region, indicating no price convergence despite substantial CBS. Five years later, survival probabilities diverge by roughly 10 percentage points across the border. I discuss the market structures that rationalize this gap between price and quantity responses.'
